# Artemis.DevTools.Viewer

Group `6_DevTools` - static library inside the game process - the 3D debug viewer: free camera, map, HUD and selection.

## Responsibility
In sparse mode (Q101) it sees only the decision ticks and does not raise the every-tick demand. The HUD shows the light ticks per second and their mean time next to the sweep time.
Draws the map and a HUD over the game frame from the published Tick: a free or game camera (the game one is the camera the game rendered the frame with, see Overlay below), the collidables, zones, fixtures and
labels, an overlay with telemetry, the panel of the selected object or the panel of the View of the watched player, and picking by ray.
It also draws that View (`ViewPass`, option `View of the watched player`, on by default): the cone of sight (`k_SightHalfAngle` and
`k_SightRange` of `Views.Raycast.Type`, from the eye along the aim, none in Full mode), a line to each player it sees, markers for its
allies, the two flags (a line to the stand when away, the carrier ringed), the radar points and the sounds, all in the color of the
relation (enemy red, ally blue, none yellow). The only thing it draws from the Tick is, in grey, the players the View does not contain, to
compare by eye with what the game shows. It also defines `EngineAccess`: the
exact members of the engine and bridge Layers the development tools may use, so they import none of those Layers.

Overlay on the game frame (VW.5a, D323, D325). With the game camera the view-projection comes from `s_render_projection` of the game (`EngineAccess::m_GameCameraService`, read at Present on the render thread, memory read only): the view from its axes and position, x and y of the projection as the game has them (m00, m11, m20, m21), the depth from the own `Lens.Near` and `Far`. The depth of the whole Viewer is reversed (VW.6a: near maps to 1 and far to 0, `MapService` clears to 0, `GpuPipeline` tests greater or equal), which also holds for the free camera. `ToScreen` and `ScreenToRay` use only x, y and w. The wireframe sits on its own mesh because the depth-only pass is pushed away from the camera, not because the lines are pulled forward. `SceneService::Update` computes once per frame the hidden set (`GetHiddenHandles`): the biped of the player whose eyes the camera is in and the objects hanging from it in the object graph. In Game mode that is the primary local player when the game hid its unit in the last closed frame (VW.6b.1: `RenderStore::WasFirstPersonUnitLastFrame`, true answers of the game's first person unit test for user 0, so the overlay hides what the game hides, one frame behind when the perspective changes), the pure `SceneService::IsOwnBipedHidden`; with Drive the detour answers false and the body is drawn, and in a vehicle, a turret, dead or in a menu nothing is expected to be hidden (the perspective is not 0 there; not measured yet). `SceneService` gets the `RenderStore` by constructor. Without the unit hook (`RenderStore::IsFirstPersonUnitHooked` false) the old rule stays as fallback: the game camera within `k_FirstPersonDistance` (0.1 wu, on foot it measures 0.004, in third person it is meters) of the View eye (`GetEyeDistance`, the same measure as the "Distance to View eye" telemetry), and with no valid game camera the camera sits on the View and it is hidden. With the Free FirstPerson follow it is the followed player. `MapService` gives the set to `DynamicPass::Upload` (both dynamic passes) and `AimPass::Upload` (spheres and center markers), and the health bars (`HealthLabels`, through `LabelContext::Hidden`) skip it too. The set is part of the upload gate of both passes (`HandleSetKeyOf`). Orbit and the free camera that follows nothing draw everything. Option `Aim spheres` (key `Viewer_AimSpheres`, on by default): off, `AimPass` draws nothing. `GetEye`, `GetForward`, `ToScreen` and `ScreenToRay` follow it, so labels and picking line up. A camera that is not valid (not finite, axes not orthonormal, m00 or m11 not positive) or no read makes the camera sit on the View of the local player as before; the free camera is unchanged.

Option `Overlay on the game frame` (key `Viewer_Overlay`, off by default) with the game camera: `MapService` does not clear the color, so the frame of the game stays under what the Viewer draws (its own depth is still cleared), the map collision is drawn in depth only (it hides what comes after it, no color) and, with `Overlay: collision wireframe` (key `Viewer_OverlayWireframe`, on by default), also as visible wireframe to check that it lines up with the walls. The other passes are the same. With the overlay off, or with the free camera, it draws as before.

Overlay under the game HUD (VW.6.x B): with the overlay active (game camera and `Viewer_Overlay`), `MapService::RenderBeforeHud` runs the whole 3D frame (scene update, uploads and every pass) from `RenderService::OnBeforeHud`, so the passes go under the game's HUD. It declines (returns false, before any update) when the overlay is not active. ImGui, labels and panels stay in `OnFrame` at Present, which calls `MapService::Render` only when `RenderStore::IsBeforeHudMarked` is false: the 3D passes and the scene update run once per frame, at one point or the other (HUD off by the game, menus, pause, `Hide game HUD`, a target of another size, a handler that declined). The labels and panels of Present use the camera, hidden set and projection computed by that one update, and the scene that update acquired (`SceneService::GetFrameScene`, one acquire per frame, D294), never a newer tick. Before the HUD the color is never cleared. If the mode changed since the decision and the overlay is no longer active after the update, nothing is drawn but the frame counts as handled. A throwing pass leaves the GPU timing closed (`GpuTimingScope`) and the frame handled. Without the overlay everything stays at Present. The telemetry line `draw:` says `scene`, `hud` or `present` (`RenderStore::GetDrawPlace`, VW.6.z) with the reason when the option Overlay in scene is on and the place is not the scene (`GetSceneStatus`, and the HUD reason at Present, `GetBeforeHudStatus`); `scene failures n` when the scene point threw.

Option `Hide game HUD` (key `Viewer_HideGameHud`, off by default, group Camera; VW.6.x): `Layer` copies it to `RenderStore::SetGameHudHidden` at the start of every Present frame, and the HUD detours of `Engine.Render` then skip the game's HUD passes (clean captures). Without the hooks installed it does nothing.

Option `Hide first person` (key `Viewer_HideFirstPerson`, off by default, group Camera; D331): `Layer` copies it to `RenderStore::SetFirstPersonHidden` at the start of every Present frame, and the first person detour of `Engine.Render` skips the game's arms and weapon. While `Drive game camera` moves the camera they are always hidden and the game draws the player's body (the unit test answers false for user 0), whatever the option says. The camera telemetry shows `First person hook: installed (model, unit, depth pass, forward)`, `third person: yes / no` and `fp unit: yes / no` (`WasFirstPersonUnitLastFrame`). `VW.6b` (hide our own biped) now follows the game, see the overlay paragraph: with Drive the overlay's own biped now matches the one of the game. Without the hooks installed it does nothing.

First person mask (VW.6.y.2 B, E, D332, always on, no option): the game's first person arms and weapon are drawn over the overlay. `Layer` copies `Viewer_Overlay` to `RenderStore::SetFirstPersonMaskRequested` at the start of every Present frame (the depth copies are only made under the overlay), and `Engine.Render` copies the depth around the first person passes of the game (0xB and 0) and puts the copies in `FrameContext::Mask`. `MapService::Draw` gives that mask to `GpuPipeline::UpdateMask` only when the overlay is active (the map view draws its own background and is never masked) and calls `ClearMask` after the passes, so every 3D pass, drawn under the HUD or at Present, skips the pixels the game writes in those passes. With `Hide first person` or Drive the game does not draw the arms and the mask is empty by itself. Without the hook the overlay draws as before. The camera telemetry has one line `FP mask: off` (not requested), `on` or `on, no copy` (neither pair was copied in the last closed frame).

Option `Overlay in scene` (key `Viewer_OverlayInScene`, on by default, group Camera; VW.6.z, D333): `Layer` copies `Viewer_OverlayInScene` together with `Viewer_Overlay` to `RenderStore::SetOverlayInSceneRequested` at the start of every Present frame. With it the same `MapService::RenderBeforeHud` handler is called from the exit of the opaque color pass of the game (0x26E500, inside the frame, after the opaque world, `RenderService::OnSceneDraw`; VW.6.z.1, D334), so smoke, shot effects, the sword blade, spawn points, projectiles and shields of the game and its HUD are composed over the overlay, with the color of the overlay under them. The overlay colors go through the exposure and tone mapping of the game, so the group Camera has the scalar (VW.6.z.2, default by D335) `Scene color scale` ( key `Viewer_SceneColorScale`, logarithmic slider from 2^-12 to 2^4, default 2^-5 = the scale of the HDR target of the game (measured: the overlay looks as with Overlay in scene off), shows the value and its log2, double click goes back to the default; its `ScalarInfo::IsLogarithmic` makes the preferences write it with 6 significant digits, not `%.3f`). The scale the overlay passes multiply their RGB by at the scene point is the slider alone; `Layer` copies it to `RenderStore::SetSceneColorSlider` every Present frame, and the scale is never used outside the scene. With Overlay in scene on the Viewer telemetry has one line `scene color scale x`. Off draws before the HUD (or at Present) exactly as before.

Option `Drive game camera` (key `Viewer_DriveGameCamera`, off by default, group Camera; VW.5b, D323): with the Free mode and nothing followed or orbited, every frame `CameraService` asks `GameCameraService::SetOverride` for the eye, forward and up of the free camera, and the observer hook of `Engine.Base` makes the game build its camera there (position, location for culling and vertical field of view the normal way, one frame later). In any other case, when the option is turned off or when the Viewer is deactivated, it calls `ClearOverride` and the camera goes back to the player. While it drives, the overlay uses the camera the game drew this frame, exactly as in Game mode (VW.5b.1): the render pose (`GetEye`, `GetForward`, `GetRight`, `GetUp`, so the view-projection, `ToScreen`, `ScreenToRay`, `GetCenterRay` and the labels) is the position and axes of the game camera read, and x and y of the projection are the game's. The game draws one frame behind the request, so rendering with the free pose would make the game's objects slide out of the overlay while the camera moves. The free pose (`m_Eye`, yaw and pitch, `GetYaw` and `GetPitch`) is the requested pose: it feeds only `SetOverride` and the flight, and is never reseated on the game camera. Without a valid game camera the render pose is the free one. `SceneService::IsGameCamera` is true and the overlay lines up with the frame. The telemetry shows `Override: on/off` and `Override faults` (only when the hook caught any fault touching the engine memory).

The overlay window (telemetry mode) shows the game camera: valid or not, position, horizontal and vertical field of view, aspect (m11 / m00) against the back buffer, the off-center terms and the distance to the eye of the View of the local player, and the line `HUD hook` (thread same or other than Present, calls per frame of the HUD draw, the render target it draws to with size and format, whether that is the back buffer, and viewport 0) from `RenderStore::GetHudHook`.

Latency measurement (VW.5c, telemetry only, nothing it measures changes what is drawn): `MapService::RenderFrame` reads the clock once per frame with the game camera (`LatencyService::ReadClock`, before the scene is chosen, VW.5d) and calls `LatencyService::Record` with it right after the scene update (under the HUD of the game when the frame is drawn there, at Present otherwise). `LatencyService` reads the clock of the engine through `EngineAccess::m_FrameClockReader` (`FrameClockReader::Read`, passing the thread context published in `CaptureStore`: TLS of the drawing thread when it has the engine context, else the published one) and pairs it with the tick of the scene that is drawn (`Match.GameTick` of the `Tick` of the `CollidableScene`, the same engine tick as `*(time globals) + 0xC`). `LatencyStore` keeps the last `k_WindowFrames` (600) frames behind one short mutex. The panel adds, after `HUD hook`, `Frame clock` (TLS yes or no, at HUD or at Present, tick T, raw fraction f, whether the game interpolates, scene tick S and lag L = T - S), `Scene lag` (histogram of L over the window: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 or more, negative, largest), `Camera moved within a tick` (n / m: of the m frames whose T equals the previous frame's, in n the game camera moved more than 1e-4 wu: if n > 0 while the player moves, the camera is interpolated) and `Interp probe` (Game mode only: with the eyes of the local player of two consecutive scenes, E(S - 1) and E(S) further apart than 0.05 wu, p50 / p99 of the distance from the game camera to E(S), to E(S - 1) and to the lerp of both with the fraction clamped to 0..1, over the frames whose scene is up to date (S == T), and how many frames had the scene behind (S < T)). `Observe` and `Summarize` of `LatencyService` are pure.

Scene of the game frame (VW.5d, D329). Measured in VW.5c at 60 fps (1 tick per frame, no effective interpolation): the frame of the game shows the state of the tick T - 1, with T the engine tick read at draw time. With the overlay over the game frame (`IsOverlayActive`), `MapService::AcquireFrameScene` therefore picks, once per frame and before the scene update, the `CollidableScene` whose `Match.GameTick` is T - 1, and the whole frame (camera, hidden set, passes, labels, panels, selection, the latency lines) comes from it. `CollidableStore` keeps the last `k_SceneHistory` (4) scenes (`Common.State.Type::SnapshotHistory`, keyed by `SceneTickOf`): `PickFor(target)` gives the exact one, else the nearest older, else the newest; `FindKey` an exact one. Without the overlay, without a valid clock or without a Match the newest is taken, as before. When the newest scene is older than T - 1, the thread that draws waits (always, no option) at most `k_FrameSceneWaitLimit` (2 ms, `Map.Type`) on the store (`WaitForKey`, a condition variable that `Publish` notifies; the store mutex is released while it waits and `Publish` only holds it to move pointers, but `MapService` keeps its own mutex during the wait, bounded by the 2 ms: it blocks `Suspend`, `Resume` and `Release` on the game thread only, never the AI thread). It does not wait if the tick did not advance since the previous frame (pause), if the pipeline is stopping (`LifecycleStore::CanPublish`), if the history does not arrive one tick at a time (sparse mode, Q101: the scenes come only on decision ticks). A scene that never comes is not waited for again within that frame: the nearest older is drawn. Above 60 fps the game interpolates between ticks: not implemented here (the interpolation below is the one of 60 fps, with the fraction of the clock).

Pose of render (VW.5e, D330). Measured in VW.5d: the camera of the game is the state of T - 1 moved on by the tick fraction f of the clock (`k = 0.04 + 1.04 f`, correlation 0.85), because the game draws interpolated between T - 1 and T. With the overlay over the game frame, the clock valid, the interpolation of the game `On` and the scene of T - 1 drawn (pick `Exact`), `MapService::UpdateFramePoses` calls `InterpolationService::Build` (`DevTools.Viewer.Interpolation.System`, the one place where it is done) with the scene shown, the scene of T if it is already in the history (it is never waited for, the 2 ms of D329 stay for T - 1) and the one of T - 2. Each dynamic collidable (matched by full handle, so index and salt) is placed by `Common.Math.System::ResolveRenderPose` between its pose in T - 1 and in T by f (`Exact`: position in a line, forward and up blended and made unit and orthogonal again), or, without T, on from T - 2 at constant velocity: pose(T - 1) + f * (pose(T - 1) - pose(T - 2)) (`Extrapolated`). Not blended (drawn as the scene shows it): an object missing in the scene used, a step past `k_InterpolationSnapDistance` (2 wu per tick: teleport, respawn, change of seat), Fixed and Phased objects (`Entity.Physics`) and objects that did not move. The result is `FramePoses`: per object the `Offset` (render position minus the position in the scene shown) and a rigid 3x4 `Transform`, with a `Revision` that changes with f and the tick so the passes that cache their upload know the poses changed. It is kept by `MapService` and copied to `SceneService` (`SetFramePoses`) for the labels. With the interpolation of the game `Off`, no clock, no overlay or a pick that is not `Exact`, the poses are empty and nothing changes from VW.5d. Uses: `DynamicPass` moves every part of an object by the `Transform` of its object when it fills the instances (CPU work per instance only, the vertices of the geometry are not touched, and the instances are rebuilt and uploaded every frame while the poses are active, as the Revision breaks the cache gate); `AimPass` moves the spheres of an object by its `Offset`; `PlayerLabels`, `ObjectLabels`, `VehicleLabels` and `HealthLabels` move their anchor by the `Offset` of their object handle (`ShiftedBy`); `ViewPass` starts the cone, the lines to the players it sees and the sound arrows from the eye of the watched player moved by the `Offset` of its biped, and `RaycastPass` starts every ray from its origin moved by it (the end stays where the scene hit); the `Interp` telemetry. Not moved: what the View of the watched player sees (other players, flags, radar markers, hidden grey markers), the distance rows of the labels, the fixture, zone, limit and interaction labels and passes, the panels (they show the values of the scene), the selection (ray against the boxes of T - 1) and the camera of the Free and Follow modes (no game frame under them). Skeletal bones are moved with the rigid move of the object, not interpolated one by one. The telemetry line `Interp (600 f): exact a  extrap b  none c` counts the frames drawn for a target tick by how the poses were found (`exact` T in the history, `extrap` only T - 2, `none` the pose of the scene as is).

## Does not
- Take part in the tick: it reads published values only, after the tick (the observers of the AI thread).
- Build the collidable scene inside the pipeline: its `CollidableSceneService` asks `Perception.Collidable.System` for it on the AI
  thread after the tick, and only while the viewer camera is active (free or game).

## Input and output
- Input: the Tick (taken with the collidable scene, published together after the tick, D294), the stores of the pure projects, `EngineAccess`, the window input of `Engine.Render`.
- Output: drawing on the game frame; the viewer options in the preferences file.

## Public interface
Other projects may import only these modules; everything else in the project is internal.

| Module | What it gives |
|---|---|
| `DevTools.Viewer.Layer` | The facade: camera, selection, overlay, options and the services that draw. While the camera follows or orbits the biped of a player, `SceneService` writes that player in the `ObservedPlayerStore` of `Services` (none otherwise), so the AI thread gives it a View (F4.2). The camera, the labels, the aim hit label and the rays and the View it draws come from the View of the watched player (`SceneService::GetObservedView`: the observed one, else the primary local player), and the free camera seats on the View of the local player. |
| `DevTools.Viewer.Access.Type` | `EngineAccess`: what the tools use of the engine and the bridge (it also carries the `FrameClockReader` of the latency measurement, the `OracleStore` of the raycast oracle and the `MapVariantDecodeStore` the bench asks decodes through), built by `Mod`. |
| `DevTools.Viewer.Camera.Type`, `DevTools.Viewer.Camera.State` | Camera modes, lens, rays and the camera input. |
| `DevTools.Viewer.Options.Type`, `DevTools.Viewer.Options.State` | Viewer options and their preference keys (`Viewer_PlayerView` is the one of the View pass, `Viewer_AimSpheres` the one of the aim spheres, `Viewer_Overlay` and `Viewer_OverlayWireframe` the ones of the overlay, `Viewer_DriveGameCamera` the one that moves the camera of the game). |
| `DevTools.Viewer.Interpolation.Type`, `DevTools.Viewer.Interpolation.System` | The poses the overlay draws the objects with between two ticks (VW.5e): `FramePoses`, `ObjectDelta`, `k_InterpolationSnapDistance` and `InterpolationService::Build`. |
| `DevTools.Viewer.Latency.Type`, `DevTools.Viewer.Latency.State`, `DevTools.Viewer.Latency.System` | The latency measurement of the overlay: window of frames, `LatencyService` (`Record`, pure `Observe` and `Summarize`) and the report the panel shows. |
| `DevTools.Viewer.Overlay.State`, `DevTools.Viewer.Selection.State` | Overlay mode (telemetry, the panels of the selected object and `View`) and page; selected object and pick requests. |

## Depends on
Every pure project, `Services`, `Bridge.Transport`, the five `Engine.*` projects and `DevTools.Gui`; it imports engine modules only
through what `EngineAccess` exposes.

## State between ticks
Camera, selection and options (saved in the preferences file; the keys never change).

## Threads
Render thread for drawing, for reading the game camera and for writing the camera override (the one writer of the `CameraOverrideStore`); window thread for input; AI thread only for the collidable scene, after the tick. The camera and option stores are atomic. `CollidableStore` readers take a short mutex (history of scenes, never held while waiting or while a dropped scene is released). `MapService::AcquireFrameScene` runs on that thread too and may wait up to 2 ms there (the game render thread under the HUD, or Present), see the scene of the game frame above. `LatencyService::Record` runs on the thread that draws the overlay (the game render thread under the HUD, or Present), the panel reads at Present: `LatencyStore` shares them with one short mutex, and the clock is read without waiting for the game thread.

## Errors
A failure in the viewer never reaches the tick; the viewer just draws nothing.

## Requirements
Never blocks the AI thread. Collidables are computed only while the viewer camera is active, outside the tick.

## Tests
`CollidableSceneTests` (the pipeline builds no scene; the scene on demand) and `SnapshotHistoryTests` (the pick of a scene by tick, the history and the wait, in `Common.State.Type`) in `Artemis.Tests`; the rest is manual.
